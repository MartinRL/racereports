## Background

A slightly delayed race report from last week's RAD. The race has now been run five times in its longest, unsupported edition, and this was likewise my fifth start. The first two years had Peter Sandholt as race director, with his hometown Horsens and the town's big arena as start and finish. A very cosy race village with plenty of grilled sausages, sweets, beer and soft drinks before the 8 PM start at two-minute intervals. Before the third edition Uggi Kaldan had taken over. So had a Chinese virus, of the whole world, so the poor guy lived in a tent next to his car at a campsite outside town, and the authorities forced us into a start spread out over half-hour slots; I got noon. For the fourth edition Uggi had moved everything a couple of dozen kilometres north of his hometown Aarhus to super-cosy Kaløvig. He had also chosen to rework the route quite a bit and, without giving it a thought, made it exactly 1,600 km long instead of Peter's 1,550 (to compensate for the time the train takes back and forth across Storebælt). Start at 4 PM at two-minute intervals last year, and like the route unchanged this year.

## Before the start

The start area is on the grounds of the genuine Gl. Løgten (an inn with five flats for rent), and with the kids now grown I had booked not only myself the night before but also my better half Friday to Saturday, plus a slightly more luxurious room in town Saturday to Sunday to do the city (which was really buzzing thanks to the Pride festival) and hang out at the finish. Start day is always the Tuesday of Ascension week.

The first four years I simply got into the family diesel van and headed for Jutland, but with the switch to an electric car it was time for a ferry debut from Sjællands Odde, saving 200 km each way (calm and pleasant out on Monday evening after work, chaos and crowds home on Sunday evening). Arrive on schedule around nine and get a lovely evening walk in the sunset with Per after my arrival, and even manage a chat with Uggi, who turned up during our walk to finish off the start and finish area.

![](bilder/race-around-denmark-2022/rad22-ferry-selfie.jpg)

Races like these should really be charged up with rest, but I have recently changed jobs and right now it is hectic, with a lot not just to get done but also many meetings, so the charging was anything but optimal with a fully booked Tuesday before the start topped off by a two-hour job interview. One has to accept that it is the job that finances the cycling and not the other way around ;-)

I have now completed countless similar races and events, and my Achilles' heel is that my stomach struggles with this kind of effort combined with the large calorie intake that is necessary. So I tested a Fortimel concept for the 24 hours before, inspired by Strasser, but could not say no to breakfast at Gl. Løgten together with Per, Anna and her partner, since a lot of this is about the social side too. Super pleasant indeed, and I went for juice and white bread with jam to be as kind as possible to the digestive system.

Per is a very dear and relatively new acquaintance. I had never met him before last year's Length of Sweden, but we rode the whole race together to a shared second-best time. 112 hours in tough conditions welds people together in a way I cannot recall since military service and similar hardships. I have since had the pleasure of hosting him and riding the Scanian 400 km club brevet together, which will soon happen again. Also great fun to see clubmate Fredrik on the start line this year in the duo category together with Günter, whom I finally got to meet in real life during that ride through Sweden. Marek I have been writing back and forth with since he showed interest in RAD last year, and not least followed his hair-raising, inhuman training regime on Strava. Fantastic to finally meet IRL, and you will search a very long time for a kinder and more humble person.

## Leg 1: to Middelfart

Finally it is 4 PM and we are released bit by bit according to the table in the race book. I start in the middle of the field. Every year I promise myself not to go out so hard at the beginning, which is easily done when you are fresh and fired up, but I know it will happen anyway with so many calves let loose on spring pasture. Sure enough, I cannot help latching on to the high pace and exchange many words with Per in particular, and after 666 km we arrive at roughly the same time at a hotel in Middelfart, though without meeting up right there. True to habit, a few dozen kilometres earlier I had bought a large night-time sandwich, two tebirkes, a smoothie and chocolate milk as supper and breakfast. The weather has been fairly nasty with a heavy downpour, so to be safe I take most things out of the bags to dry before shower and bed. I aim for 4 h 15 min of sleep but get only 3 h 43 min, and my knees hurt badly when I get up to pee after about two hours. That has only happened to me in tough conditions in the Italian mountains during equally long races, with mechanical problems that had taken away the easy gears. Very worrying, and unfortunately pain that lasts the whole race; as I write this I cannot even ride to work without my right knee hurting, while the left seems to have healed. I suspect new shoes, pedals and cleats caused it, since it was very hard to unclip once rain and grime had done their work.

## Leg 2: Zealand

I get going around two in the morning, and by then both Marek and Per are far ahead, having chosen either not to sleep or to sleep considerably fewer hours. It is important, though, to know your limits and ride your own ultra race, which is a very big difference from a road race where, on the contrary, it is all about constantly reading the field and weighing tactics. Wind and temperature are favourable, but unfortunately wet. Either from above but as a rule from the road surface, or rain from below as I call it. The plan is to reach a hotel on Zealand 542 km away in 24 hours. The Fortimel diet has worked surprisingly well, but now the strain takes its toll on the stomach. On top of that my tracker stops working, which means many calls and chats, not least with Uggi. Time-consuming, and the last thing you want to spend your by now limited cognitive abilities on. Unclear how or why, but after I-do-not-know-how-many restarts of the tracker (which are a bit more involved than a simple button press) it comes back to life after a few hours, and the car on its way with a replacement can turn back to Jutland.

I am really sluggish and seek company with Formiddag med Simpson for music chat and lovely music on P6 Beat. Then suddenly I get lots of cheering and ❤, first from Lars Fly who treats me to a Coke, then RAD 2020's great heroine Elin Starup turns up with her partner, then rando buddy Rasmus Krog and not least Steen Linder-Madsen, who not only comes out by car but later also pays a visit by velocipede in the dark, and I learn most of what there is to know about the Danish efterskole system, among other things. I have great respect for the rest of the journey to the hotel in northern Zealand, as I can no longer count the times the Sandman has come with his dose of melatonin and you fall asleep at the bars. Naturally the heavens open again and it is literally like cycling in a shower with proper Felix-annoy-an-engineer pressure. Saved by a pleasant old wooden bus shelter and a five-to-ten-minute power nap, and it is half past three in the morning when I, tired, wet and cold, skid into the hotel and force down two energy bars and a Fortimel, shower and get into bed. I am now far from both dream time and the fight for the top, and know that the next day will offer a 17 m/s headwind to Jutland via Funen, so I make the wise decision to again set the alarm for 4 h 15 min of sleep (which likewise again becomes somewhat less, 3 h 31 min), fully aware that my original plan has come to nothing and that Mr Sandman will inexorably come surfing on the wave of the circadian rhythm the coming night too.

## Leg 3: home across Funen

Since I am behind plan there is the option of a hotel breakfast, and I set out to get the flies' worth of calories in and sort out the stomach in one go, which works. The forecast looks promising and I get going at a quarter past nine with far too few clothes. Why does one never learn about wind chill? Nyborg station has a large, well-stocked 7-Eleven, and as on the way in the other direction I stock up here, take the chance to eat, visit the toilet and fill up on water, and am as ready as one can be for the hard leg across the little island. On Funen I freeze like a dog in the strong headwind despite up to 20 °C on the Garmin screen. The knees get worse and worse, especially the right one, so it is hard to put out power, which inexorably punishes you with slow cycling.

Finally across Lillebælt and back on Jutland soil! Now just the last 300-odd hilly kilometres to conquer to be at the finish in Kaløvig! Naturally a gigantic downpour in the early dark hours, and these are also the race's coldest hours, well below 10 °C. Just as I had expected, it eventually becomes impossible to stay awake at the bars. Here my homemade cue sheet is a great help, as I know that 67 km into the last leg there is a church with an open toilet. It is tiny, but has both a lukewarm radiator and a doormat, so I curl up in the corner wrapped in my thermal foil blanket (which I always carry in case of an accident). My watch is unable to register this sleep, but I think I sit there for about an hour.

## Finish

I wake up fresh as a daisy ;-) and all that remains is to pedal to Kaløvig and bring home the silver nice and calm (Per sadly having withdrawn) on the sore knees, since there is neither anyone to chase nor anyone on my heels. Magical to see my fantastic wife at the finish in the early morning together with Uggi, and after some good chat with them, a shower and an equally magical breakfast at Gl. Løgten topped off by the well-rested winner Marek's entrance and a longer talk with him, which probably served as a much-needed debriefing for him too. Then two hours of sleep, beers with Meagan and Per, welcoming Fredrik and Günter, and on into Aarhus for lots of food, wine and "hygge" :D
